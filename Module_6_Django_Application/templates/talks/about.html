Personalized TED Talks Recommendation System

Academic & Engineering Milestone Documentation: Modules 1 through 6

Problem Statement

TED curates a large and diverse collection of talks spanning technology, science, education, business, healthcare, culture, creativity, and global challenges. However, users frequently struggle to navigate this extensive repository to discover talks that closely align with their personal interests and conceptual curiosity.

The objective of this project is to build an end-to-end, reproducible, modular, explainable, and production-ready recommendation system that extracts rich multi-modal textual signals from TED Talk content and delivers relevant, diverse, and transparent recommendations.

Recommendation Strategies

1. TF-IDF Content-Based

Extracts multi-field n-grams across Talk Title, Description, Tags, and Full Transcripts, producing a sparse 44,613-dimensional feature space with field-weight optimization. Excels at exact keyword, speaker, and terminology matching.

2. Sentence Transformers

Encodes talk content into 384-dimensional dense semantic vectors using all-MiniLM-L6-v2. Captures deep conceptual themes, synonyms, and latent topical analogies without requiring exact vocabulary overlap.

3. Multi-Signal Hybrid + MMR

Combines lexical similarity, semantic embeddings, and user topical boosts. Applies Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR, $\lambda=0.70$) with sparse matrix slicing to prevent redundancy and boost intra-list diversity by +20.6%.

Research Integrity & Collaborative Filtering

An empirical audit of the public TED dataset confirmed that the archive supplies aggregate talk metrics (views, comments, ratings) and contains zero individual user identifiers or interaction logs.

Academic Transparency: In adherence to scientific integrity, no synthetic users or fake interaction matrices were fabricated. Collaborative filtering is formally reported as unsupported by available evidence. The personalization demonstrated in this system is driven by explicit session interest vectors and multi-talk seed centroids.
